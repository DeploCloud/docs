title: Take over your VPS
description: Install Deplo on the machine that already runs Dokploy or Coolify. It brings your projects across, takes the ports, and removes the old panel when you say so.
---

<Callout type="info" title="Beta">
  This feature is currently beta/experimental and subject to change. It is not
  recommended for production. Try it out and share your feedback on [our
  GitHub](https://github.com/DeploCloud/deplo/issues).
</Callout>

Most people have one server, and it already runs something. Deplo installs on
that server by **taking its place**: it brings your projects across, then takes
ports 80 and 443 from the old panel.

Your domains keep pointing at the same IP, and Deplo inherits the certificates
that panel already had. Nothing to change in DNS, no HTTPS gap.

<Callout type="warn">
  On a machine that already runs Dokploy or Coolify, this is the **only** way
  Deplo installs. Two panels cannot share ports 80 and 443, so the installer
  either replaces the other one or exits without touching anything.
</Callout>

---

## What you need

- A machine running Dokploy or Coolify, with root access for one command.
- An API token from that panel. Deplo reads it over the API, the same way
  [Move from Dokploy](/docs/guides/move-from-dokploy) and
  [Move from Coolify](/docs/guides/move-from-coolify) do.
- Room on the disk: every volume that comes across is written a second time
  before the old one goes.

---

## The takeover

<Steps>

<Step>

### Run the installer

The same command as any other install:

```bash
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DeploCloud/deplo/main/install.sh | bash
```

It finds the other panel and asks:

```
[ !] Dokploy is already running on this machine.
     Deplo can only install here by taking its place: it brings your projects
     across, then removes Dokploy. Two panels cannot share 80 and 443.

  -> Migrate off Dokploy and let Deplo replace it? [y/N]
```

Answer **no** and nothing is installed. Answer **yes** and Deplo installs on a
free port, leaving the other panel exactly where it is.

`--yes` does not answer this one: replacing somebody's platform is not a
default. To install without being asked, set `DEPLO_TAKEOVER=dokploy` (or
`coolify`).

</Step>

<Step>

### Leave the window open

The installer prints the address to open and then waits. It is the half of this
that needs root on the host: moving the ports, restarting Docker, removing the
old panel.

Closing it is safe. Re-run `/opt/deplo/install.sh` to pick the takeover back up.

</Step>

<Step>

### Create your account and migrate

Open the address it printed. After the account and the team, Deplo opens the
migration wizard and nothing else: until the ports are its own, there is
nothing to deploy onto.

The panel it is replacing is already filled in. Paste its API token, pick what
you want to bring across, and start.

<Callout type="warn">
  What you leave unticked stays behind, and goes when the old panel is removed.
</Callout>

</Step>

<Step>

### Take the ports

Press **Take over the ports** when everything you want is here. In the terminal
window, Deplo:

- stops the old panel, without removing anything
- copies its certificates into its own store
- moves Traefik onto 80 and 443, with the dashboard served behind it
- restarts Docker, which applies the address pools the install wrote

The dashboard answers on its own HTTPS address from then on: your domain if you
gave one, and the generated `deplo-<hex>.nip.io` either way.

</Step>

<Step>

### Deploy your apps

Deplo never deploys anything for you. Your apps arrived with their
configuration and their data, and you deploy them one at a time, checking each
one.

The old panel is stopped but still on the disk, volumes and all. That is your
way back for as long as you want it.

</Step>

<Step>

### Remove the old panel

When you are happy, **Remove Dokploy** (or Coolify) on the takeover screen. You
type its name to confirm, and its containers, the workloads it ran, their
volumes and networks, its images and its directory all come off the machine.

</Step>

</Steps>

## If the dashboard does not open

Deplo waits on a free port, and some providers only open 22, 80 and 443 in
their own firewall. The installer notices that nothing has reached the panel
and offers two ways in that need no port opened:

<Accordions>

<Accordion title="Through the old panel's proxy, on port 80">

Deplo drops one route into the other panel's Traefik, which reloads it by
itself, and prints an address like:

```
http://deplo-setup.203.0.113.10.sslip.io
```

Nothing else of theirs is touched, and the route is removed when the ports
move.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Over SSH, from your own machine">

```bash
ssh -L 3001:localhost:3001 root@<server-ip>
```

Then open `http://localhost:3001`. Nothing on the server changes.

</Accordion>

</Accordions>

---

## Backing out

**Cancel and remove Deplo** on the takeover screen, at any point before the
ports move. Deplo:

- starts again every service the migration stopped on the old panel
- removes everything it created here
- uninstalls itself

The old panel keeps all of its data. It needs its API token again to start
those services, so keep the one you pasted.

If the port move itself fails, the installer puts the ports back and starts the
old panel again on its own.

---

## What it never does

- **Deploy anything for you.** Some apps need a change after a migration, so
  every deploy is yours to make.
- **Remove anything before you ask.** Taking the ports stops the old panel; it
  is still whole on the disk until you confirm the removal.
- **Touch a machine you said no on.** Answering no to the first question exits
  before anything is written.
